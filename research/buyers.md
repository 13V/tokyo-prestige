# Who buys from a JDM importer in Australia, and what the site must do for them

Built from the evidence files in `evidence/` (gathered 11 November 2026 by three research agents):
`forums.md` (88 quotes, mostly Skylines Australia, Lexus Owners Club Australia, ProductReview and Ford
Forums), `reviews-social.md` (YouTube comments on four import videos, dealer-hosted testimonials) and
`import-rules.md` (the rules and the terminology). Quotes are word for word, typos kept. Anything
marked *(inference)* is my reading, not something a buyer said.

## Limits of this evidence, stated first

- **Reddit, Whirlpool, OzBargain and Club Lexus blocked the browser**, so there is no evidence from them.
- **No Tokyo Prestige review could be read.** Google Maps would not show the review panel, Google Search
  showed a captcha, and their Facebook page shows no reviews when logged out. Their site quotes none. So
  nothing here says what Tokyo Prestige's own customers value; that needs their Google Business Profile.
- Most readable forum threads are from 2009 to 2016; a few are from 2020 to 2025. They show lasting
  worries, not today's prices.
- People-mover evidence leans on reviews of one Sydney dealer (Best People Movers) and on comments under
  one Alphard/Vellfire review, some of them from overseas viewers.
- No forum mention of Tokyo Prestige was found, good or bad. *(inference)* Their own site and their
  Google profile are the only places a careful buyer can form a view of them.

## What every profile shares

1. **They arrive suspicious.** "ALMOST EVERY car yard winds back odos and does dodgy shit. It is just a
   part of import life in Australia." (Nightcrawler, Adelaide, 2014,
   [SAU](https://sau.com.au/forums/topic/444073-dealership-question)). "With the high incidence of
   odometer tampering on imports by sellers, it's hard to know what's genuine these days." (a competing
   importer, 2016,
   [SAU](https://www.sau.com.au/forums/topic/467299-get-past-japan-auction-info-and-km-history-for-most-imports/)).
2. **Paperwork is the honesty test.** "ask for the dereg papers and export certificate. if they baulk at
   this.. walk away.. If they are an honest dealer, they will have no problems supplying you these
   documents" (sonicii, 2014, [SAU](https://sau.com.au/forums/topic/444073-dealership-question)). "If the
   dealer can't show you the auction sheet, walk away." (Alkatraz, 2011,
   [SAU](https://www.sau.com.au/forums/topic/368522-is-it-just-me-or-are-all-used-car-yard-importers-dodgy/)).
3. **They choose a person, not a logo.** "I have been dealing with Troy from Iron chef and he has been
   outstanding." (Black Dog, 2014, [SAU](https://sau.com.au/forums/topic/444073-dealership-question)).
   "He's a great guy who is transparent and honest." (@banialjawi on
   [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kQJA3TPwtgM)).
4. **Insurance comes up late and kills deals.** "Big insurance companies won't go near imports. Try
   Hard2Place, Shannons, or Just Car Insurance." (Howiefied, 2011,
   [Lexus Owners Club](https://au.lexusownersclub.com/forums/topic/9844-toyota-harrier-insurance-australia)).
   "No insurance company accepts japanese car man thats really disappointing." (@yaseencaptain1883,
   [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GnIEa0rEQso)).

## Profile 1: the enthusiast who knows the chassis code

**Who.** Knows a BNR34 from an ER34 and a GT-B E-tune from a GT-B. Has wanted the car for years. Often
owns another import. Reads the forums and will check a VIN.

**What they search for.** The model code and grade, not the dealer: "BNR34 for sale", "JZS171 Athlete V",
"R33 Autech 40th", "Crown Athlete VX", "Legacy B4 RSK manual". *(inference from how they write on forums;
no search data was available.)*

**What they fear.**
- A repaired wreck: "a quick auction sheet request revealed that despite it had a very low genuine km, it
  was a complete wreck. ... the A pillar and other chassis parts were cut and welded back together"
  (j-2007, 2014, [SAU](https://sau.com.au/forums/topic/449808-buying-a-r34-gtr-m-spec)).
- Forged papers: "I almost got burnt with a car in Sydney that had photo shopped auction papers showing
  90,000 less kms" (Ange_34, 2014, same thread).
- A dealer who does not know the car, or dodges: "he started telling me every place grades them
  differently and that they don't mean anything and thus did not tell me." (gersfan, 2009,
  [SAU](https://sau.com.au/forums/topic/272933-import-dealer-car)).

**What they need to see before they enquire.** The auction sheet and grade; the export or deregistration
certificate; the VIN or frame number; underbody photos; who did the compliance work ("Based on RAWS, car
was complied on the 12 August 2014 ... by BARREV PTY LTD", j-2007); modifications listed; honesty about
flaws. "When buying a JDM car, check the auction grade, with at least 4B for newer models and no XX ...
parts on the sheet. Check the odometer verification website." (@numberfive-t5c,
[YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kQJA3TPwtgM)).

**What makes them pick a dealer.** Correct terminology, documents offered before being asked, and a named
person who knows the model. One wrong chassis code and the dealer is written off. *(inference, supported
by the "walk away" quotes above.)*

**What this means for the site.** Model code and grade on every card and at the top of every car page,
set like a spec plate. The VIN or frame number shown. A "documents" block that says plainly what exists
for this car and offers it on request. No generated copy.

## Profile 2: the family buyer after a people mover

**Who.** A parent replacing a seven-seat SUV. Not a car person. Has researched for a long time: "I've
been wanting to buy a Toyota Tarago imported from Japan for the past two years." (alfama, VIC,
[ProductReview](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/best-people-movers)).

**What they search for.** "Alphard for sale Adelaide", "Vellfire hybrid", "Voxy hybrid 7 seater", "Japanese
import people mover". *(inference.)*

**Why they buy.** "I used to laugh at why people would want a car like this ... Then I have 2 kids and I
bought one because I need the space. Best decision ever." (@santochow,
[YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvPg7b-wwL8)). "Best car ever Toyota alphard ... best for our
kids for school ever days!!" (Andy H., NSW,
[ProductReview](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/best-people-movers)).

**What they fear.**
- Compliance that affects the family directly: "Nothing but a headache concerning the seating capacity
  certification and the missing 3 child restraint anchors" (@robertaitken3048,
  [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rSNg8c51RmY)).
- The Japanese screen: "The only problem is the language on the entertainment system. Is it possible to
  change ? And the other thing is the map which is still stuck in Osaka" (@manifestsingh5910, same video).
- Being out of their depth: "we were still confused and I had a list of 12 questions" (Pete, NSW,
  ProductReview). "we knew nothing before speaking with Natalia" (Pamela and Peter Summers, ProductReview).
- Rust and mileage: "I am amazed how rusty some of the Estimas are in videos ... I also, was very worried
  about mileage tampering" (@brettsalter3300, YouTube, same video).

**What they need to see.** Seats and how they are certified, child-seat anchor points, whether the screen
and navigation work in English, the hybrid system and warranty, a third-party inspection ("Recent RACV
inspection advised us the best Elgrand they have seen!", Simon, VIC, ProductReview), and plain answers.

**What makes them pick a dealer.** Being taught without being sold to: "Great family business focusing on
educating customers about Japanese imports." (Jenia M., ProductReview). Patience: "Natalia had the
patience to hold TWO vans for a few weeks whilst I worked out my finances" (Brett S., TAS, ProductReview).

**What this means for the site.** A "people mover" filter and a seats fact on the card. A plain-English
importing page. On the car page, the family questions answered in a fixed block (seats, anchor points,
screen language), marked "ask us" where Tokyo Prestige has not published the answer. A "hold this car"
request.

## Profile 3: the luxury-for-less buyer

**Who.** Comparing a 2020 Crown G-Executive at $48,888 or an LS 500 at $68,888 with a new mid-range car.
Cares about condition, kilometres and comfort more than heritage.

**What they search for.** "Toyota Crown for sale", "Lexus LS 500 used", "Crown hybrid Australia", and the
price bracket on a marketplace. *(inference.)*

**Why they buy.** "I couldn't have bought the same car here - not available here - and I couldn't find
one in anywhere near the condition with such low ks." (@Thorold63,
[YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kQJA3TPwtgM)). "The car was so much cleaner and well kept than
any on the Aussie market." (@Feeder2-h6g, same video). "Looks like you get alot of car for the money
you're paying in comparison to the local RAV4, Tiquan, Murano etc." (MP_R33, 2014,
[SAU](https://www.sau.com.au/forums/topic/441399-skyline-crossover)).

**What they fear.**
- Parts and servicing: "PLEASE dont buy a grey import.... they are nightmares when it comes to parts
  availability." (Interceptor, 2023,
  [Ford Forums](https://www.fordforums.com.au/showthread.php?t=11497952)). Against that: "any brake parts
  from the 3rd gen GS350 and 2005+ IS350 will fit the X" (ilv1004s, 2013,
  [Lexus Owners Club](https://au.lexusownersclub.com/forums/topic/11854-new-member-toyota-mark-x-grx133-350s)).
- Insurance: "The one place that has offered insurance will cover maximum $23k for over $900 premium."
  (Goofy, 2011, Lexus Owners Club).
- No history on an expensive car: "It is an expensive car (well over $50K), and it seems strange that
  anyone would be so blasé about the lack of service history or related documents." (foibles, 2016,
  [SAU](https://www.sau.com.au/forums/topic/466771-confirming-japanese-sales-data)).
- The dealer's margin: "Don't buy from dealers. You're paying the mark up because you can't wait."
  (@JC94, [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kQJA3TPwtgM)).

**What they need to see.** Kilometres with proof, service history, what the price includes, a repayment
figure, who services it afterwards, and a reason to pay a dealer rather than a broker.

**What makes them pick a dealer.** The car is here, complied and can be driven today. That is Tokyo
Prestige's own line ("Skip the lengthy import process and any guesswork") and it is the direct answer to
@JC94. In-house servicing answers the parts fear. *(inference.)*

**What this means for the site.** "In Adelaide now, drive it this week" as the status on every available
car. A repayment estimator on the car page. The servicing workshop shown on the car page, not only on
its own page. Price with a plain note on on-road costs.

## Profile 4: the interstate buyer who will never visit the yard

**Who.** Any of the three above, living in another state. "I live in another state, so I can't check them
out personally." (Black Dog, Riverland, 2014, SAU).

**What they fear.**
- Losing a deposit: "Now I've put a deposit on this sight unseen (it's in Sydney and I'm in Adelaide)" ...
  "the dealer has told me a lot of porkies and refuses to give my deposit back" (lesleyjill, 2015,
  [SAU](https://www.sau.com.au/forums/topic/452610-oops-i-been-stoopid-anybody-fancy-a-trip-to-homebush-pleez)).
- Paying a stranger: "What is the best way to pay them without them running off with the money" (PerthR33GTST,
  2022, [SAU](https://sau.com.au/forums/topic/483471-wondering-best-way-to-purchase-sight-unseen-rolling-shell)).
- Freight cost and registration at home: "transport can range from 400-800bux depending on the type but
  make sure insurance is added" (Johnny, 2009,
  [SAU](https://www.sau.com.au/forums/topic/274700-buying-interstate)); "Import/compliance requirements
  vary from state to state." (@BrianCroy-t1j, [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GnIEa0rEQso)).
- A warranty that means nothing far from the dealer: "it came with a nationwide warranty but I've since
  discovered that it ain't worth the paper it's written on." (lesleyjill, 2015).

**What they need to see.** Photos of everything: "took huge photos in high resolution of every panel of
the car even underneath it." (steveo_r33, 2009, SAU). A video. An independent inspection they arrange
themselves: "I will ring RAA & get them to send an independent mechanic to look over the vehicle for me."
(Black Dog). How delivery works, and what they must do to register the car at home. People do buy this
way when it is done well: "We bought our JDM AWD 2006 Estima from Natalia, sight unseen, last year and it
hasn't missed a beat." (MicheleD, QLD, ProductReview). A viewer asked the reviewers outright: "Would love
to see you guys do a video for interstate car buying" (@datingsimplifiedofficial, YouTube).

**What makes them pick a dealer.** A licence number they can check, a street address, a named person who
answers, and a dealer who invites the independent inspection rather than tolerating it. *(inference.)*

**What this means for the site.** "Request a walk-around video of this car" on every car page. A delivery
section on the car page that repeats Tokyo Prestige's own FAQ answers (freight to door or depot, insured
in transit; buyer organises roadworthy and registration at home). "Independent inspections welcome", in
their words ("Third-party inspections: Ensure your peace of mind"). Licence number and address in the
sticky bar's sheet and the footer.

## Ranked: what a buyer needs before they will enquire

Ranked by how often it came up across the evidence. The ranking is mine.

1. Proof of kilometres and history (auction sheet, export or deregistration certificate).
2. Enough photos to judge condition, including underneath, and a video for remote buyers.
3. A named, knowledgeable person and a business they can verify.
4. Exact identity of the car: model code, grade, VIN or frame number.
5. What the price includes, and what happens with registration in their state.
6. Insurance, parts and servicing answered.
7. A way to hold the car or be told when one arrives, without committing.

Tokyo Prestige's current site delivers part of 4 and none of the rest on the car page (see `client.md`).

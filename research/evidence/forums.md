## Synthesis (my synthesis, not quotes)

Ranked by how often the theme appeared across the threads I could read (about 15 SAU threads plus ProductReview, Lexus Owners Club, ozvr4, Ford Forums). Counts are rough; the sample is small and mostly pre-2017.

1. **Odometer and condition fraud is the dominant fear (appeared in most threads).** The default assumption is that yards wind back odometers or hide accident history. Phrases like "almost every car yard" and "walk away" are normal. (inference) A buyer arrives at a dealer's website already suspicious, so the site has to answer the fear before the enquiry.
2. **Paperwork as the test of honesty.** The same checklist repeats: auction sheet, export certificate / deregistration papers, and a mention that a refusal means "walk away". Buyers also know sheets can be faked, so some want a third-party history check. A free mileage check was cited by a buyer as what separated a real dealer from a scammer.
3. **Independent inspection.** Posters repeatedly say to pay for a third-party check, including remotely and interstate. Photos of "every panel ... even underneath" were mentioned as a good practice.
4. **Trust in a named person or a long track record.** Positive dealer experiences name a person (Iron Chef's "Troy", "Natalia"). Reviews praise being educated, patience (holding a car), transparent documents and after-sale help. Negative reviews are about vanishing after payment.
5. **Insurance and parts.** Insurance refusal, agreed-value doubts and high premiums come up in every era (2011 to 2025). Parts worries come from outside the enthusiast bubble. (inference) A page that says who insures these cars and what shares parts with local models would remove a late-stage objection.
6. **Interstate and sight-unseen risk.** Deposit safety, freight cost, warranty not worth it, and getting a refund. This is relevant to Adelaide-based buyers shopping Sydney yards and to out-of-state buyers of Adelaide stock.
7. **Dealer-name memory and rebrands.** Buyers track dealers across renamed businesses and warn each other. Reputation is public and long-lived.
8. **Why they buy: cheap for the spec, low km, Japan-only models, family comfort.** Present but lighter than fears. Family buyers (Estima/Elgrand) stress comfort, large family seating and long research before contacting a dealer.

Gaps and cautions: no Reddit or Whirlpool data (blocked); nothing on 25-year-rule buyers, Alphard/Vellfire owners, Japanese nav, resale or Tokyo Prestige; old thread dates mean fewer current prices; many posters are enthusiasts, who are more sceptical than family buyers (inference).

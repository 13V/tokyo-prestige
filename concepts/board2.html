The last set put a gimmick on top of an ordinary page. These start the other way round: each takes one thing the research found that buyers need and no dealer does well, and builds the page from the best working example of it on Mobbin. Every source is named on its page.

Direction J · mock-up

The figures

Every car is introduced by the same four figures in the same order: year, kilometres, gearbox, price. They are set large and light with small units, above one wide photo. A slim bar keeps the price and the actions in reach.

From the research

  • Finding 4: the best listings keep a fixed order of facts on every car (Cars & Bids).
  • Finding 10: premium is restraint, with big figures beside a big price (Classic Throttle Shop, Porsche).
  • Client audit: their first price is 8,070px down the phone homepage. Here it is in the first screen.
  • Pattern 3: price and the main action pinned on a phone.

From Mobbin

  • Rivian, figures over the car: four large light numerals with small units, and a pill that switches model.
  • Rivian, R1T: a slim bar with the name, the price and two actions.
  • Turo, car page (iOS): price left, one button right, fixed to the bottom.

Colour

Type

Geist, light figures.

Homepage, desktop

Strengths

  • The four things a buyer asks first are read before anything else
  • One grammar from hero to card to car page: easy to scan 62 cars
  • Light and calm; works with their photos as they are

Risks

  • Kilometres are set as large as the price, which is honest and may not flatter a 204,322 km car
  • Quiet: it will not win on drama
  • Needs every listing to have all four figures (some coming-soon cars list no gearbox)

Direction K · mock-up

The lineup

There is no hero. The homepage is the stock, shown the way a maker shows a range: a short heading, then large tiles two across, each with the same labels in the same corners and a tag on its edge for In Adelaide, Coming soon or Under contract.

From the research

  • Finding 10: Classic Throttle Shop and Romans win on uniform photos. Tokyo Prestige already shoots every car from the same angle in the same room; this uses it.
  • Pattern 8: for sale, coming soon and reserved on one page (Torque GT).
  • Gap 7: coming-soon stock is a list, not a hook. Here each has “tell me when it lands” in place of a price.
  • Gap 2: nobody gives the car a clean first screen on a phone.

From Mobbin

  • Shupatto, lineup: a Japanese maker’s range page. Large pale tiles, a statement top left, the model top right, a vertical tag on the edge, dots bottom left.
  • Face Formula, All products: one large serif heading, then the products.

Colour

Type

Newsreader, Albert Sans.

Homepage, desktop

Strengths

  • The most distinct of the three, and the most Japanese without a single Japanese cliché
  • Photos are large: the car sells itself
  • Coming-soon cars sit in the same list and collect enquiries

Risks

  • Two across means a long page for 62 cars: needs a compact view and the filter close by
  • No hero means no place for a message beyond the heading
  • Shows any inconsistency in their photography

Direction L · mock-up

Start from what you need

The homepage asks what the car has to do and answers from the stock: four doors by need, a budget slider drawn from their real prices, and body types with a count and a lowest price. The list underneath changes as you choose, with no reload.

From the research

  • Buyer profiles: the family after seven seats, the luxury-for-less buyer with a budget, the enthusiast who wants a manual. Each gets a door.
  • Patterns 1 and 2: search on the first screen with a live count; filters that never reload.
  • Client audit: all nine of their stock filters read “No options”.
  • Every number shown is counted from the 62 cars for sale: 5 with seven seats or more, 31 under $30,000, 6 manuals, 10 under 30,000 km.

From Mobbin

  • Cosmos, shop: photo doors with a serif italic label and a View pill.
  • Turo, price filter: a histogram of prices over the slider, and a button that carries the count.
  • Kiwi.com, car results: type tiles with a from price.
  • Shift: search in the header.

Colour

Type

Instrument Serif, Inter.

Homepage, desktop

Strengths

  • Likely the best of the three for enquiries: it shortens the path for the buyer who does not know model names
  • The histogram shows at a glance that most of their stock is under $40,000
  • The doors can change with the stock and the season

Risks

  • Dark, like direction A; could be made light
  • Doors must be kept honest as stock changes (counts are live, so an empty door is hidden)
  • Least suited to the enthusiast, who goes straight to search

My pick: K for the look, with L's tools.

K is the one that looks like nobody else selling cars in Australia, and it does it with what they already have: one room, one angle, every car. L's budget slider and need doors are the best enquiry tools here and would sit naturally above K's tiles as the filter.

J is the safest.

It is the easiest to judge at a glance and the hardest to dislike. Its four-figure grammar could also be taken into the built site (direction A) without changing A's look.

What is not proven.

These are two screens each, drawn at 1440 and 390 wide, with no motion and no working filters. K and L have not been tried with all 62 cars in the page.
